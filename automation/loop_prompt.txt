## IMPORTANT — what local arena testing is and isn't for

Local arena testing gave a misleading signal once (back in the Iwaparesu era): a change made purely because real Kaggle replay data proved it fixed a real, verified loss pattern *lowered* the local win rate against our strongest local sparring bot — yet the change was still correct and worth shipping. The local bot pool is narrow, mostly homegrown, and not representative of the real diverse Kaggle field. As of the 2026-08-08 pivot, `agents/archive/baseline` (and the main `iwaparesu_yoshida_v2` sparring agent) run an Iwaparesu-style wall ability that hard-counters this deck's `ex`-flagged Mega Lucario ex attacks by design (~10-20% observed win rate there is expected, not a regression signal) — prefer `baseline2`/`baseline3`/`baseline4`/`sakaki` for a more representative local read, though even those remain a narrow, non-representative pool. Win-rate swings against any of them (even 5-10 points) do not reliably indicate whether a change helps or hurts on Kaggle.

**So: local arena testing is a bug/crash safety net only — not a go/no-go quality gate.**
- Run a quick smoke test (20-50 games is enough) against `agents/archive/baseline` after any code change, purely to confirm `errors: 0` (no crashes/exceptions) and that games complete normally.
- Do NOT block a submission just because local win rate is flat, lower, or not obviously improved. Only treat local testing as a real red flag if something is obviously broken — a crash, an exception, or a win rate collapsing toward ~0%.
- The primary evidence for whether a change is good is the real replay-data analysis in step 2/3 below, not local arena win rate.
